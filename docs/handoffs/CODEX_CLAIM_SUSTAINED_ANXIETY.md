# Codex claim: sustained anxiety without cue spam

The owner wants players worried about dying through much of the session. Codex
claims director/config pacing and focused tests for this pass. The opening dread
remains protected through 3:39 total, the spawn zone remains protected, and the
quiet audio direction remains intact.

The target is a bounded post-dread uncertainty cycle: short recovery after real
danger, then rising pressure if players have gone too long without a glimpse,
close freezing presence or committed pursuit. Helium Voice should repay overdue
pressure rather than filling the gap with skunk appearances or more ambient
alarms. Actual attacks retain line-of-sight, physical movement, flashlight
counterplay and cooldown constraints.

Initial baseline: eight real 15-minute scripted sessions (two seeds across four
player behaviours) exposed long unresolved periods. The harness then revealed
that its safe-gap metric omitted real appearances and most of the freezing aura;
the corrected paired baseline and result are recorded below.

## Result

The useful solution was more **resolved uncertainty**, rather than more damage
or more warning sounds. The baseline already placed Helium Voice inside the
outer freezing field for roughly half of a typical session, and five of eight
scripts ended in death. Raising damage or adding another heartbeat/breath cue
would shorten play and turn fear into a constant range meter.

The director now grants its 75–130 second recovery interval only after a player
actually perceives an appearance. A plan that cannot start retries after 6–12
seconds. An accepted plan gets a bounded 48-second reservation to travel and
resolve; if it stays unseen, the director can try again instead of treating the
failed plan as a scare. Deliberate appearances now choose Helium Voice 82% of
the time, never choose the skunk twice in succession, and exclude the skunk
when close presence is overdue.

The pacing harness now measures the game's actual warning language: a real
appearance and Helium Voice inside the 25 m inner freezing ring both break a
safe gap. It previously omitted director-reported appearances from this metric
and counted only the 10 m critical ring, which overstated silence despite the
owner's freezing-aura rule. Minute formatting also no longer prints `4:60`.

Paired eight-session results, same behaviours and seeds, 15-minute cap:

| Measure | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Perceived Helium Voice appearances | 12 | **20** |
| All deliberate appearances | 20 | **22** |
| Hunt starts | 18 | **23** |
| Time in active hunts | 117 s | **860 s** |
| Helium Voice hits | 18 | 20 |
| Sessions ending in death | 5 | 6 |
| Median longest safe gap | 3:56 | **3:44** |

The scripted hider deliberately never looks around, so visual appearances are
not guaranteed for that case; close freezing and physical hunts still resolve
its pressure. The opening remains protected through 3:39 total. Damage,
heartbeat, breathing, footsteps, spawn exclusion, navigation,
flashlight counterplay and line-of-sight attack rules are unchanged.

## Owner follow-up: more volatile hunts

Hunts now change pace every 0.9–2.3 seconds within a bounded 0.88–1.18 speed
multiplier. Each hunt also samples its unseen cutoff delay (0.65–1.25×), and hunt
cooldowns vary between 0.65–1.18× instead of arriving at one learnable interval.
Cooldowns/recovery are shorter and physical commitment begins within 42 m.

A survived Helium Voice appearance schedules a physical hunt attempt after a
hidden 10–28 second delay. It still uses his real approach. The paired sample
therefore has 23 hunts instead of 18. Focused hunt tests retain the facts that a
sprinting player cannot outrun him, a trunk cannot block the final strike, and
the close flashlight counter ends a hunt.

On reaching the player, he now physically prowls a 12–15 foot ring for a sampled
26–34 seconds before the final rush. The timer accumulates only while he is
actually nearby; running away makes him catch up before the prowl continues.
This increased active hunt exposure from 117 to 860 seconds in the paired
sessions while hits rose only from 18 to 20. The seven-foot flashlight counter
remains available if a player chooses to charge toward him during the prowl.

If a teammate opted into mimicry and has an unused phrase in memory, 58% of
eligible hunts may play that teammate's copied voice 0.6–2 seconds after the
hunt begins. The emitter is placed 16–30 m away, broadly opposite Helium Voice's
real bearing. It never uses the hunted player's own phrase for this trick and
never runs without a consenting teammate phrase. The clip remains one-use,
memory-only and server-authorized.

When the nearby hunt hum starts, the cold/frost/temperature target fades to
zero. The broad non-directional hum tells the player a hunt is active without
preserving the cold distance gauge. Cold returns after the hum/hunt resolves;
only physical footsteps inside 6–8 feet provide precise close audio.
